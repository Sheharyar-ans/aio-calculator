Calorie and Macro Calculator

A calorie and macro calculator determines your Basal Metabolic Rate, Total Daily Energy Expenditure, and optimal distribution of daily calories across protein, carbohydrates, and dietary fats. Tailoring macronutrient ratios ensures you maintain athletic energy levels and preserve lean muscle mass while achieving targeted body composition and fitness goals.

Formula and Calculation Method

Mifflin-St Jeor Equation for Basal Metabolic Rate (BMR):

Men: BMR = (10 × weight in kg) + (6.25 × height in cm) - (5 × age) + 5
Women: BMR = (10 × weight in kg) + (6.25 × height in cm) - (5 × age) - 161
TDEE = BMR × Activity Multiplier (1.2 to 1.9)

Macronutrient Energy Density: Protein = 4 kcal/g, Carbohydrates = 4 kcal/g, Fats = 9 kcal/g.

Worked Example and Step-by-Step Calculation

Fitness Macro Plan (Male, 28 yrs, 180 lbs / 81.6 kg, 5 ft 11 in / 180 cm, Moderately Active, Muscle Gain)
Input Values:
  • Sex: Male
  • Age: 28
  • Height: 180 cm (5 ft 11 in)
  • Weight: 81.6 kg (180 lbs)
  • Activity Level: Moderately Active (exercise 3-5 days/week: 1.55 multiplier)
  • Goal: Lean Muscle Gain (+250 kcal surplus)

1. BMR = (10 × 81.6) + (6.25 × 180) - (5 × 28) + 5 = 816 + 1125 - 140 + 5 = 1,806 kcal

2. TDEE = 1,806 × 1.55 = 2,800 kcal / day maintenance

3. Target Calories (Surplus) = 2,800 + 250 = 3,050 kcal / day

4. Protein (1.0 g/lb = 180g): 180g × 4 kcal = 720 kcal (23.6%)

5. Fats (0.4 g/lb = 72g): 72g × 9 kcal = 648 kcal (21.2%)

6. Carbs (Remaining): (3,050 - 720 - 648) ÷ 4 kcal = 1,682 ÷ 4 = 420g carbs (55.2%)

Result: Daily Target: 3,050 kcal | Protein: 180g (720 kcal) | Fats: 72g (648 kcal) | Carbs: 420g (1,682 kcal)

Common Mistakes, Limitations & Practical Guidance

  • Physical activity multipliers are estimates; real metabolic rates vary based on Non-Exercise Activity Thermogenesis (NEAT).
  • Individuals with kidney or liver conditions should consult their physician before adopting high-protein dietary protocols.

Scientific Standards and Clinical Metabolic Guidelines

Physiological estimates generated by this application align with evidence-based standards published by the World Health Organization (WHO Technical Report Series 854), the National Institutes of Health (NIH), and the American College of Sports Medicine (ACSM). Caloric requirements and basal metabolic rates are calculated using peer-reviewed anthropometric formulas, specifically the Mifflin-St Jeor equation, which clinical reviews validate as the most accurate predictor of resting metabolic expenditure in healthy adults.

Body composition analyses incorporate the U.S. Navy circumference method (NAVPERS 6110/5), providing body density estimates that correlate strongly with dual-energy X-ray absorptiometry (DEXA) scans while eliminating equipment barriers for home users.

Three Practical Health and Fitness Application Scenarios

Scenario 1: Moderate Weight Management Protocol

An individual with a maintenance expenditure of 2,200 calories per day aims for sustainable body fat reduction. Establishing a moderate 400-calorie daily deficit results in a daily target of 1,800 calories. Over a 12-week cycle, this consistent deficit yields an accumulated deficit of 33,600 calories, producing approximately 9.6 pounds of fat loss while preserving metabolic rate.

Scenario 2: Athletic Recomposition and Macronutrient Target

A 75 kg resistance-trained athlete maintaining at 2,500 daily calories structures intake around lean tissue retention. Following International Society of Sports Nutrition (ISSN) guidelines of 2.0 grams of protein per kilogram body weight, the athlete consumes 150 grams of protein (600 kcal), 70 grams of dietary fats (630 kcal), and 317 grams of carbohydrates (1,270 kcal).

Scenario 3: Sedentary to Active Lifestyle Progression

A sedentary office worker with a Basal Metabolic Rate of 1,600 kcal increases daily activity from sedentary (1.2 multiplier = 1,920 kcal TDEE) to moderately active (1.55 multiplier = 2,480 kcal TDEE) through 8,000 daily steps and three weekly workouts. This behavioral shift burns an extra 560 kcal daily without altering dietary intake.

Frequently Asked Questions About Calorie and Macro Calculator

What is the optimal protein intake per kg for body composition?

For active individuals and resistance trainers, research recommends 1.6 to 2.2 grams of protein per kilogram of body weight (0.73 to 1.0 g/lb) to stimulate muscle protein synthesis and prevent lean tissue loss.

What are macronutrients?

Macronutrients (macros) are the three primary nutritional categories your body requires in large quantities for energy and survival: proteins (tissue repair and enzymes, 4 kcal/g), carbohydrates (primary fuel for brain and muscles, 4 kcal/g), and dietary fats (hormone production and cellular health, 9 kcal/g).

How much protein do I need per day?

The Recommended Dietary Allowance (RDA) minimum is 0.8g per kg of body weight (0.36g/lb) to prevent deficiency. For active individuals and resistance trainers seeking muscle preservation or growth, exercise science consensus recommends 1.6 to 2.2g per kg (0.7 to 1.0g per pound).

What is the Mifflin-St Jeor formula?

Published in 1990 in the American Journal of Clinical Nutrition, the Mifflin-St Jeor formula is clinically validated as the most reliable predictive equation for measuring Basal Metabolic Rate (BMR) in non-athletic and athletic adults.

Does this page include a standalone TDEE mode?

Yes. This calculator incorporates a full integrated TDEE calculation mode. You can also visit our dedicated TDEE Calculator page for standalone resting vs active energy breakdowns.

Can I track macros without counting calories?

Because every gram of protein and carb contains 4 calories and fat contains 9 calories, tracking your macros automatically controls your caloric intake while ensuring optimal nutritional balance.
