Investment and ROI Calculator

An investment ROI calculator measures the profitability and performance efficiency of an investment by computing total Return on Investment (ROI) and annualized ROI (Compound Annual Growth Rate). It enables investors to compare the financial performance of real estate, equities, and business ventures across varying holding periods.

Formula and Calculation Method

Simple ROI and Annualized ROI formulas:

Net Profit = Final Value - Initial Cost
ROI (%) = (Net Profit ÷ Initial Cost) × 100
Annualized ROI (%) = [(Final Value ÷ Initial Cost)^(1/t) - 1] × 100

Worked Example and Step-by-Step Calculation

Real Estate / Stock Sale ($60,000 invested, $95,000 returned after 4 years)
Input Values:
  • Initial Investment Cost: $60,000
  • Final Value Returned: $95,000
  • Holding Period: 4 years

1. Net Profit = $95,000 - $60,000 = $35,000

2. Simple ROI = ($35,000 ÷ $60,000) × 100 = 58.33%

3. Annualized ROI (CAGR) = [($95,000 ÷ $60,000)^(1/4) - 1] × 100

4. [1.583333]^0.25 - 1 = 1.12175 - 1 = 12.17% per year

Result: Net Profit: $35,000 | Simple ROI: 58.33% | Annualized ROI: 12.17% / year

  • Verify Input Units: Confirm whether terms are expressed in monthly versus annual intervals, or metric versus imperial units, to avoid order-of-magnitude scaling errors.
  • Account for Compounding Cycles: Nominal interest rates yield higher effective yields when compounded monthly or daily rather than annually.
  • Model Multiple Scenarios: Always evaluate conservative, expected, and stress-tested cases before executing binding agreements or life decisions.

Frequently Asked Questions About Investment and ROI Calculator

What is the difference between simple ROI and annualized ROI?

Simple ROI tells you the total percentage gain over the entire holding period, regardless of whether it took 6 months or 10 years. Annualized ROI normalizes the return on a per-year basis, allowing you to accurately compare a 50% gain over 5 years against a 12% gain over 1 year.

What is considered a good ROI on an investment?

In the stock market, an annualized ROI of 7% to 10% is historically considered solid. For venture capital or speculative real estate developments, investors typically target 15% to 25%+ annualized ROI to compensate for higher capital risk.

How is ROI different from profit margin?

ROI measures the return generated on invested capital (gain divided by investment cost). Profit margin measures the portion of gross revenue remaining after operating expenses (profit divided by total revenue).

How do dividends affect ROI?

Any dividends or rental income received during the holding period should be added to the final return value, as they represent realized cash gains that increase total ROI.
