Amortization Calculator

An amortization calculator generates a complete schedule of periodic loan payments, detailing the exact allocation of every installment toward principal repayment versus interest charges over time. In the early stages of a loan, payments consist predominantly of interest, while later payments shift progressively toward reducing principal.

Formula and Calculation Method

For each payment period t:

Interest_t = Remaining Principal_{t-1} × r
Principal_t = Monthly Payment - Interest_t
Remaining Principal_t = Remaining Principal_{t-1} - Principal_t

Worked Example and Step-by-Step Calculation

Month 1 vs Month 180 on a $300,000 Loan (6.0% for 30 Years)
Input Values:
  • Loan Principal: $300,000
  • Interest Rate: 6.0%
  • Tenure: 30 years (360 months)
  • Monthly Payment: $1,798.65

1. Month 1: Interest = $300,000 × (0.06 ÷ 12) = $1,500.00. Principal = $1,798.65 - $1,500.00 = $298.65. (83.4% interest / 16.6% principal).

2. Month 180 (Year 15): Remaining balance is $215,831. Interest = $1,079.16. Principal = $719.49.

3. Month 360 (Final): Interest = $8.95. Principal = $1,789.70. (99.5% principal).

Result: Total Repaid: $647,514.57 (Principal: $300,000 | Total Interest: $347,514.57)

Common Mistakes, Limitations & Practical Guidance

  • Models standard fixed-rate amortizing loans; balloon payments and interest-only structures follow different repayment logic.
  • Escrow fees (taxes, insurance) are excluded from basic loan amortization schedules as they do not reduce loan principal.

Frequently Asked Questions About Amortization Calculator

How does principal vs interest change over time in an amortization schedule?

Early loan payments consist predominantly of interest because the unpaid balance is highest. As principal decreases each month, the interest portion shrinks and the principal portion expands, creating an inverted curve over the loan term.

What is a loan amortization schedule?

A loan amortization schedule is a complete chronological table showing each scheduled periodic payment, detailing exactly how much money goes toward interest charges, how much reduces principal, and the ending loan balance.

Why is interest higher at the beginning of an amortization schedule?

Interest is charged directly as a percentage of the remaining principal balance. Because your balance is highest at the start of the loan, the interest charge is at its peak. As you chip away at principal, subsequent interest charges shrink.

How do extra principal payments affect the amortization schedule?

Extra payments bypass interest entirely and reduce the principal balance directly. This shortens the loan term, accelerates subsequent principal payoff, and cuts total interest paid over the life of the loan.

How does the Amortization Calculator differ from the Mortgage Calculator?

The Mortgage Calculator includes escrow items like property tax, homeowners insurance, and PMI to estimate your total monthly housing cost. The Amortization Calculator focuses purely on the mathematical loan schedule (principal vs interest balance).

Yes. Our tool provides an instant copyable summary and annual breakdown view suitable for personal financial records and loan comparisons.
