Geometry Calculator

A geometry calculator computes the geometric properties of 2D shapes (area, perimeter, circumference) and 3D solids (surface area, volume). By selecting a shape—such as a circle, rectangle, triangle, sphere, cylinder, or cone—and providing known dimensions, it calculates all corresponding measurements instantly.

Formula and Calculation Method

Key 2D and 3D Geometric Formulas:

  • Circle: Area = πr², Circumference = 2πr
  • Rectangle: Area = l × w, Perimeter = 2(l + w)
  • Triangle: Area = ½ × base × height
  • Cylinder: Volume = πr²h, Total Surface Area = 2πr(r + h)
  • Sphere: Volume = (4/3)πr³, Surface Area = 4πr²
  • Cone: Volume = (1/3)πr²h, Slant Height s = √(r² + h²)

Worked Example and Step-by-Step Calculation

Cylinder Dimensions: Radius = 4 cm, Height = 10 cm
Input Values:
  • Shape: Cylinder
  • Radius (r): 4 cm
  • Height (h): 10 cm

1. Base Area = π × (4 cm)² = 16π ≈ 50.27 cm²

2. Volume = Base Area × Height = 50.27 × 10 = 502.65 cm³

3. Lateral Surface Area = 2 × π × 4 × 10 = 80π ≈ 251.33 cm²

4. Total Surface Area = 2πr² + 2πrh = 2(50.27) + 251.33 = 351.86 cm²

Result: Volume: 502.65 cm³ | Total Surface Area: 351.86 cm²

Frequently Asked Questions About Geometry Calculator

What is the difference between surface area and volume?

Surface area measures the total 2-dimensional area covering the outside of a 3-dimensional solid (measured in square units like cm²). Volume measures the internal 3-dimensional space or capacity enclosed within the object (measured in cubic units like cm³).

What is the Pythagorean theorem?

The Pythagorean theorem states that in any right-angled triangle, the square of the hypotenuse (the side opposite the right angle) equals the sum of the squares of the other two sides: a² + b² = c².

How do you calculate the volume of a sphere?

The volume of a sphere is given by V = (4/3) × π × r³, where r is the radius of the sphere. For example, a sphere with a 3-inch radius has a volume of (4/3) × π × 27 ≈ 113.1 cubic inches.

How do you convert cubic centimeters to liters?

One liter equals exactly 1,000 cubic centimeters (cm³ or mL). For example, a cylinder with 2,500 cm³ volume holds exactly 2.5 liters of liquid.
