BMI and Ideal Weight Calculator

Body Mass Index (BMI) is a screening metric that evaluates body weight relative to height to assess whether an individual falls into an underweight, normal, overweight, or obese classification. For adults, a healthy BMI ranges between 18.5 and 24.9 according to the World Health Organization (WHO), while ideal body weight formulas estimate target healthy weights based on biological sex and height.

Formula and Calculation Method

Standard Body Mass Index (BMI) formulas:

Metric: BMI = weight (kg) ÷ [height (m)]²
Imperial: BMI = [weight (lbs) ÷ (height (in))²] × 703

Devine Ideal Body Weight (IBW) Formula:

  • Men: IBW (kg) = 50.0 kg + 2.3 kg × (height in inches - 60)
  • Women: IBW (kg) = 45.5 kg + 2.3 kg × (height in inches - 60)

WHO Adult BMI Classifications: Underweight (< 18.5), Normal weight (18.5 – 24.9), Overweight (25.0 – 29.9), Obesity Class I (30.0 – 34.9), Class II (35.0 – 39.9), Class III (≥ 40.0).

Worked Example and Step-by-Step Calculation

Adult Male: 5 ft 10 in (70 inches / 1.778 m) weighing 172 lbs (78.0 kg)
Input Values:
  • Height: 5 ft 10 in (70 inches / 1.778 m)
  • Weight: 172 lbs (78.0 kg)
  • Biological Sex: Male

1. Metric BMI = 78.0 ÷ (1.778)² = 78.0 ÷ 3.1613 = 24.67 kg/m²

2. Imperial Check = [172 ÷ (70)²] × 703 = [172 ÷ 4900] × 703 = 24.68 kg/m²

3. Category = Normal Weight (Healthy) (18.5 to 24.9 range)

4. Devine Ideal Body Weight = 50.0 + 2.3 × (70 - 60) = 50.0 + 23.0 = 73.0 kg (160.9 lbs)

5. WHO Healthy Weight Range for 5 ft 10 in = 128.9 lbs to 173.5 lbs (58.5 kg to 78.7 kg)

Result: BMI: 24.7 (Normal Weight) | Devine Ideal Weight: 161 lbs | Healthy Range: 129–174 lbs

Common Mistakes, Limitations & Practical Guidance

  • BMI does not distinguish between muscle mass and fat tissue; muscular athletes and bodybuilders may register as "overweight" or "obese" despite very low body fat.
  • BMI does not measure visceral fat distribution around abdominal organs, which carries higher cardiovascular risk.
  • Not calibrated for pregnant women, growing children/teens (who require CDC age-growth percentiles), or the elderly.

Frequently Asked Questions About BMI and Ideal Weight Calculator

Does the BMI formula differ for men and women?

The standard WHO formula (weight in kg / height in meters squared) is identical for adult men and women, but healthy body fat percentages and body composition distribution vary significantly between biological sexes.

What is considered a healthy BMI range?

The World Health Organization (WHO) and Centers for Disease Control and Prevention (CDC) define a normal, healthy adult BMI as 18.5 to 24.9 kg/m². BMIs below 18.5 are classified as underweight, 25.0 to 29.9 as overweight, and 30.0 or above as obese.

Why does BMI overestimate risk for muscular individuals?

BMI relies solely on height and total weight. Because muscle tissue is substantially denser than adipose fat tissue, athletic individuals with high muscularity often have high BMIs despite possessing low, healthy body fat levels.

What is the Devine formula for ideal body weight?

Developed in 1974 by Dr. Ben Devine, the formula calculates baseline ideal weight starting at 50 kg for men and 45.5 kg for women at 5 feet tall, adding 2.3 kg for each additional inch of height. It is widely used in clinical pharmacology for medication dosing.

How does this tool differ from the BMI and Body Fat Calculator?

While this tool calculates height-to-weight BMI and theoretical ideal weight, our BMI and Body Fat Calculator incorporates body circumference measurements (neck, waist, hips) to estimate true body fat percentage and lean muscle mass.

How much weight do I need to lose to drop one BMI point?

For an average adult height of 5 ft 10 in (178 cm), approximately 6.5 to 7.0 pounds (3.0 to 3.2 kg) of weight loss will reduce your BMI by exactly 1.0 point.
