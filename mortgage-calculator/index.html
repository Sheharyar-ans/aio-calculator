Mortgage Calculator

A mortgage calculator computes your monthly loan payment by factoring in home purchase price, down payment, interest rate, and repayment term. It provides a full breakdown of principal, interest, property taxes, homeowners insurance, and private mortgage insurance, revealing total interest costs across the loan amortization schedule.

Formula and Calculation Method

The total monthly mortgage payment (PITI + PMI) equals:

Monthly Payment = P&I + (Annual Property Tax ÷ 12) + (Annual Home Insurance ÷ 12) + Monthly PMI
P&I = Loan Amount × [r(1+r)^n] ÷ [(1+r)^n - 1]

Worked Example and Step-by-Step Calculation

Home Purchase Example ($450,000 Home, 10% Down, 6.54% 30-Year Fixed)
Input Values:
  • Home Price: $450,000
  • Down Payment: $45,000 (10%)
  • Loan Amount: $405,000
  • Interest Rate: 6.54% (Editable market reference)
  • Property Tax: 1.2% / yr ($450/mo)
  • Homeowners Insurance: $1,440 / yr ($120/mo)
  • PMI: 0.7% of loan / yr ($236.25/mo)

1. Principal & Interest (P&I) on $405,000 at 6.54% for 30 years = $2,570.54

2. Property Tax = ($450,000 × 0.012) ÷ 12 = $450.00

3. Home Insurance = $1,440 ÷ 12 = $120.00

4. PMI = ($405,000 × 0.007) ÷ 12 = $236.25

5. Total Monthly Payment (PITI + PMI) = $2,570.54 + $450.00 + $120.00 + $236.25 = $3,376.79

Result: $3,376.79 / month (P&I: $2,570.54 | Tax: $450.00 | Ins: $120.00 | PMI: $236.25)

How to Use the Mortgage Calculator

  1. Enter the target purchase price of the home.
  2. Input your cash down payment as a dollar amount or percentage.
  3. Review or edit the current interest rate (defaulted to latest market average).
  4. Choose your loan term (15 or 30 years).
  5. Add expected property taxes, insurance, and HOA dues to inspect your true all-in monthly payment.

Common Mistakes, Limitations & Practical Guidance

  • Interest rates are user-editable inputs. Lenders price mortgages based on personal FICO scores, debt-to-income ratios, and property type.
  • Property tax rates vary widely by county and municipal reassessment schedules.
  • PMI automatically drops when loan-to-value reaches 78-80% under the federal Homeowners Protection Act; this calculator models upfront PMI.

Frequently Asked Questions About Mortgage Calculator

How does a 15-year mortgage compare to a 30-year mortgage with extra payments?

A 15-year fixed loan carries a slightly lower interest rate and forces rapid equity building, but requires higher monthly cash flow. Alternatively, adding extra payments to a 30-year mortgage gives you payment flexibility while achieving similar interest savings.

What is the mortgage payment formula and what does PITI mean?

The standard mortgage payment formula calculates monthly Principal and Interest: M = P[r(1+r)^n] / [(1+r)^n - 1]. PITI represents Principal, Interest, property Taxes, and homeowners Insurance: the four components of your complete monthly housing draft.

What does PITI stand for in a mortgage payment?

PITI stands for Principal, Interest, Taxes, and Insurance. Principal repays the borrowed loan balance; Interest is the lender finance charge; Taxes represent local municipal property taxes held in escrow; Insurance covers homeowners hazard protection.

When is Private Mortgage Insurance (PMI) required?

On conventional loans, PMI is typically required whenever a buyer puts down less than 20% of the home purchase price. It protects the lender in case of default and generally costs between 0.3% and 1.2% of the initial loan amount annually.

Should I choose a 15-year or 30-year fixed mortgage?

A 30-year mortgage offers lower monthly payments, giving you cash flow flexibility. A 15-year mortgage has higher monthly payments but generally carries lower interest rates and saves hundreds of thousands in lifetime interest charges.

How do property taxes affect my mortgage payment?

Most mortgage servicers collect 1/12th of your estimated annual property taxes each month in an escrow account, then pay the local taxing authority on your behalf when property taxes come due.
